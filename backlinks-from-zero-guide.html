Step 1

Set up your project and connect your tools

  1. Open Claude and add the seo-backlinks and humanizer skills in Settings → Capabilities. These are the brain and the voice of the whole system.
  2. Make a project, name it "Backlinks From Zero," and paste the instructions below into Project Settings → Instructions.
  3. Sign up free at Source of Sources so journalist requests land in your inbox. It is the free service built by the original HARO founder.
  4. Verify your site in Google Search Console (free, one time). This is what the Tracker reads.
  5. Add a free Bing Webmaster Tools account too, it catches links Google misses.
  6. Connect Google Search Console. Pick one of three ways: the GSC MCP (free, open source, self-host), Windsor.ai (no-code, about 1 minute, paid with a free trial), or Composio (managed auth, free tier, for builders).
  7. Get a free Moz Links API key for authority scores. Free tier is plenty to start.
  8. Optional, for competitor and visibility depth: add the DataForSEO MCP and top up a little at dataforseo.com.

Honest note: foundation links are table stakes, they make you findable but will not rank you alone. They are the floor. The editorial links in move two are what climb.

2 · The Pitch the links that move rankings

Editorial links, from roundups and journalists, are what actually climb. Claude finds the "best tools" roundups and the reporters asking for sources right now, and drafts a pitch that does not read like spam.

Prompt
Scan today's Source of Sources digests and any "best [my niche] tools 2026" roundups. Find the 5 most relevant to [brand]. For each, draft a short, humanized pitch using the humanizer skill, with one real proof point: [proof]. Do not send anything, just draft each one so it does not read like spam.

Speed matters: popular Source of Sources requests get hundreds of replies within the first hour. You reply from your own inbox, before the deadline. Never auto-blast.

3 · The Tracker

A live board wired to your own Search Console and Bing (and DataForSEO if you want depth). It watches your links, your visibility, and your competitors' link gaps, and flags every change on its own.

Prompt

A Foundation tab (the claimable links with copy-ready submissions), a Pitch tab (roundups and journalist requests with drafted pitches), and a Tracker tab wired to my Google Search Console and Bing. Show backlinks landed, my Domain Rating trend, my organic visibility, and my top 3 competitors' referring domains as a link gap I can pitch. Refresh the numbers each time I open it.

Step 3 · make it run itself

Put the Tracker on autopilot

An artifact only refreshes when you open it. So schedule one tiny task to watch your links, visibility and competitors every week, even when you are not looking.

Prompt
Every Monday at 9am, use my Google Search Console connection (and DataForSEO if set up) to check for new and lost backlinks and my visibility trend. Compare against these 3 competitors: [comp 1, comp 2, comp 3]. Message me a short summary of what changed, and flag any link that dropped.

Now the board climbs on its own, and you get pinged the moment a link drops.

Step 4 · make it a real, linkable asset

Host your Backlink Board on GitHub, free

GitHub Pages hosts a single HTML file as a live web page, for free. This gives you a real link to your own tool, which is itself a linkable asset (the whole point of move two). Here is the exact path.

  1. Create a free account at github.com. Skip if you have one.
  2. Click the + top right, then New repository. Name it something clean like backlink-board, set it to Public, tick Add a README, and click Create repository.
  3. Rename your zero.html to index.html on your computer first (this makes the URL clean). Then in the repo click Add file → Upload files, drag in index.html, and Commit changes.
  4. Go to Settings → Pages. Under Source pick Deploy from a branch, set the branch to main and the folder to / (root), then Save.
  5. Wait about a minute and refresh. GitHub shows your live URL: https://[your-username].github.io/backlink-board/. That link is now a live, shareable tool.
  6. To update it later: open index.html in the repo, click the pencil to edit (or re-upload), and Commit changes. Pages redeploys on its own within a minute.

Optional: put it on your own domain

In Settings → Pages, add a custom domain like tools.yoursite.com, then add the matching CNAME record with your domain provider. Now your tool lives on your brand, which is a stronger asset than a github.io link, and a better thing to point pitches at.
